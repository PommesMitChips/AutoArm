[h1]Getting Started with AutoArm[/h1]

Build your arm, name its base and head, then let AutoArm discover the joints between them. Finish the setup within 5 minutes.

[h2]1. Place your first actuator[/h2]

Place a rotor, hinge, or piston on your grid. This first actuator will be the base of your arm.

[img]{{IMAGE_01_ROTOR_ON_GRID}}[/img]

[h2]2. Name the base[/h2]

Open the rotor in the terminal and name it [b]<MyArmName> - Base[/b]. Replace [b]<MyArmName>[/b] with your arm's name; for example, [b]Arm 1 - Base[/b].

[img]{{IMAGE_02_NAME_BASE}}[/img]

[h2]3. Build the rest of the arm on top[/h2]

Build from the base rotor toward the tool. Intermediate rotors, hinges and pistons can keep their ordinary names.

The example, from bottom to top, is:

[olist]
[*]Base rotor.
[*]A crossbar.
[*]Two hinges, one on each end of the crossbar, with a shared rotation axis.
[*]Two pistons, one above each hinge.
[*]A second crossbar, joining the piston heads.
[*]A single hinge in the middle of the upper crossbar.
[*]Rotor → hinge → piston → rotor → hinge → hinge → rotor → drill head.
[/olist]

[img]{{IMAGE_03_ARM_FRONT}}[/img]

[h3]What not to do[/h3]

Parallel hinges and pistons are supported when their stages line up.

[b]Parallel stages that don't line up:[/b] Parallel placement is supported, but offset hinge and piston stages are not supported.

[img]{{IMAGE_03A_MISALIGNED_STAGES}}[/img]

[b]Hydraulic structures:[/b] A piston used as a brace to drive a hinged linkage is not supported.

[img]{{IMAGE_03B_HYDRAULIC_LINKAGE}}[/img]

[h2]4. Name the head[/h2]

Name the tool or final terminal block at the moving end [b]<MyArmName> - Head[/b]. Use the same prefix as the base: [b]Arm 1 - Head[/b] in this example.

[img]{{IMAGE_04_NAME_HEAD}}[/img]

[h2]5. Place a cockpit or controller[/h2]

Place a cockpit, control seat or another ship controller on the same construct. Sit in it, or take control of it, when you are ready to send movement inputs.

[img]{{IMAGE_05_CONTROLLER}}[/img]

[h2]6. Place a programmable block[/h2]

Place a programmable block on the same construct as the arm. This block will run AutoArm. The example keeps it beside the arm on a connected foundation grid.

[img]{{IMAGE_06_ARM_AND_PROGRAMMABLE_BLOCK}}[/img]

[h2]7. Load the script and run [b]On[/b][/h2]

[olist]
[*]Open the programmable block's [b]Edit[/b] window.
[*]Click on Browse Scripts.
[*]Select the script from your list.
[*]Enter [b]On[/b] in [b]Argument[/b], then press [b]Run[/b].
[/olist]

For a fresh programmable block, AutoArm discovers arm names from the base markers and generates its configuration.

[img]{{IMAGE_07_RUN_ON}}[/img]

[h2]8. Take control of your arm[/h2]

Sit in your cockpit or take control of your ship controller. Use [b]WASD[/b] and [b]C / Space[/b] to move the arm. By default, movement follows the head's orientation.

[list]
[*][b]W / S[/b] — Forward / backward
[*][b]A / D[/b] — Left / right
[*][b]Space / C[/b] — Up / down
[/list]
